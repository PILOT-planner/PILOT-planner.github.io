Abstract

Latent world-model planning has shown promising results in goal-conditioned robotic manipulation, but remains challenging for long-horizon planning due to limited temporal guidance and inefficient action search. A distant goal image offers little guidance for preparatory actions, capturing intermediate subgoal images by hand is impractical, and uninformed sampling makes action search inefficient. We propose PILOT (Policy-Initialized Local Optimization with Temporal subgoals), which brings two foundation models into latent world-model planning: a video model turns a language instruction into visual subgoals, and a vision-language-action policy initializes the cross-entropy method (CEM). CEM refines action sequences toward each subgoal with an action-conditioned world model, and no goal image is needed at test time.

With both models fine-tuned on 40 demonstrations, PILOT achieves 68.8% success on four real UR5e tasks, compared with 55.0% without policy initialization, 13.8% for policy-only execution, and 0.0% for direct final-goal planning. In simulation, PILOT exceeds direct execution of the same policy by 30 percentage points on long Push-T tasks and by 22 percentage points on LIBERO-10.

Subgoals and the policy prior are complementary: better subgoals and stronger priors each raise success, and even a weak policy that succeeds alone on only 3.3% of medium Push-T tasks is an effective prior.

PILOT framework

A language-conditioned video model, a VLA policy, and an action-conditioned world model,
connected by a closed-loop planner.

Implementation details
Wan2.1-1.3B

Predict temporal subgoals

Generate a future video from the initial observation and language instruction. Select uniformly spaced frames as intermediate planning targets.

π0 + CEM

Initialize and optimize

Initialize the CEM mean with an action proposal conditioned on the current observation and instruction. Refine candidates using the active subgoal as the planning target.

JEPA-WM + MPC

Execute and replan

Evaluate candidate actions in frozen DINOv3 feature space. Execute a short action prefix, observe the new state, and replan before advancing to the next subgoal.

Real-world control: 6 visual targets · prediction horizon h = 2 · 1 action executed per update · 2 planning–execution cycles per target.

Planning efficiency and the strength of the policy prior +

Even a weak policy is an effective prior: the policy that succeeds alone on only 3.3% of medium tasks raises the fitted planning success plateau from 34% to 48%. Stronger priors improve both the plateau and the time needed to reach it. The 30-demonstration prior exceeds 60% success at 8 seconds per planning call. Policy initialization reaches in four iterations the cost that generic initialization reaches in ten, and finishes 24.1% lower under the same sampling budget.

Policy initialization improves final-goal planning on short tasks (33.3% → 46.7%), while success remains 0.0% on medium and long tasks. Imagined subgoals and the policy prior together improve execution across all task lengths.

Short: expert episode length < 100 steps. Medium: 100–129. Long: ≥ 130. One world-model step corresponds to five environment steps; the prediction horizon stays fixed.
